## 1. Executive Summary & Thesis Problem Statement

Automotive radar operating in the 77–81 GHz frequency band represents a mission-critical sensor modality for Advanced Driver Assistance Systems (ADAS) and Autonomous Vehicles (AVs) due to its operational robustness under degraded optical conditions (heavy fog, rain, snow, direct sun glare, and complete darkness). However, contemporary radar perception systems face a severe foundational dilemma at the edge:

1. **The Classical Signal Processing Bottleneck**: Classical radar digital signal processing (DSP)—relying on Fast Fourier Transforms (FFT), Cell-Averaging Constant False Alarm Rate (CA-CFAR) detection, spatial super-resolution (MUSIC, ESPRIT), and clustering (DBSCAN)—suffers catastrophic performance collapse below $0\text{ dB}$ Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR). In dense multi-target scenarios and heterogeneous clutter environments, classical CFAR suffers from threshold inflation (target masking), while MUSIC exhibits covariance rank deficiency and computational explosion ($O(M^3)$ eigendecomposition), yielding non-deterministic execution times ($15\text{--}50\text{ ms}$) that violate hard real-time automotive deadlines ($<5\text{ ms}$).
2. **The Deep Learning Computational Paradox**: Recent advances in deep neural spectrum sensing (e.g., RadDet ICASSP 2025, RAVEN CVPR 2026) demonstrate that deep convolutional backbones (such as YOLOv9-C with Programmable Gradient Information) break classical Rayleigh resolution boundaries, achieving superior target detection ($25.93\%\text{--}47.37\%\text{ mAP}_{50}$ on dense multi-target radar spectra at $-20\text{ dB}$ SNR where CFAR yields $<5\%$). However, state-of-the-art radar deep learning relies on **monolithic, static-resolution inference**: every radar frame—regardless of whether it contains an empty open highway or a crowded urban intersection—is processed at maximum spatial-frequency resolution ($512\times 512$, demanding high FLOPs) or at a degraded uniform resolution ($128\times 128$), inducing substantial accuracy penalties on complex scenes.
3. **The Proposed Thesis Contribution**: This thesis resolves this fundamental trade-off by establishing the paradigm of **Computationally Efficient Radar Spectrum Detection via Adaptive Computation**. Rather than treating radar spectrum detection as a static feed-forward computation, we dynamically modulate neural inference depth, spatial-frequency resolution, and feature routing conditioned on real-time radar scene complexity, local SNR tiers, and prediction uncertainty.

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                               THE ADAPTIVE COMPUTATION PARADIGM                                    |
|                                                                                                    |
|  Incoming 77 GHz FMCW Radar Frame  ---> [ Stage 1: Ultra-Fast Triage (128x128) / 1.12 ms ]        |
|                                                     |                                              |
|                                      Uncertainty & SNR Gate?                                       |
|                                                     |                                              |
|                           [Confident / High SNR] <--+--> [Ambiguous / Low SNR / Dense Targets]     |
|                                     |                                         |                    |
|                        Emit Fast-Path Detections                  Escalate to High-Fidelity        |
|                            (~80% of frames)                       Cascade / Deep Backbone Heads    |
|                        Avg Latency: ~1.4 ms                       (256x256 / 512x512)              |
|                                                                                                    |
|  Result: High-resolution accuracy on difficult frames with low-resolution latency on clear frames  |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 2.1. RadDet (Zi Huang et al., IEEE ICASSP 2025)
* **Dataset & Benchmark Scope**: RadDet introduces a real-world, 40,000-frame wideband radar dataset acquired with a 500 MHz bandwidth transceiver ($f_c = 500\text{ MHz}$, $T_c = 50\ \mu\text{s}$, $1\times 10^6$ ADC samples/frame). It evaluates 11 distinct signal modulation classes (LFM, FMCW, Rectangular, Barker, Frank, P1–P4, Px, Zadoff-Chu) across 6 calibrated SNR tiers ($-20\text{ dB}$, $-12\text{ dB}$, $-4\text{ dB}$, $+4\text{ dB}$, $+12\text{ dB}$, $+20\text{ dB}$).
* **Data Transformation**: The raw radar time-domain signal is converted into a 2.5D real-valued max-hold spectrogram across three standard resolution tiers:
  * **S-128 ($128\times 128$)**: Downsampling pooling factor 75, temporal resolution $\Delta t = 15.6\ \mu\text{s}$, frequency resolution $\Delta f = 3.91\text{ MHz}$.
  * **M-256 ($256\times 256$)**: Downsampling pooling factor 19, temporal resolution $\Delta t = 7.81\ \mu\text{s}$, frequency resolution $\Delta f = 1.95\text{ MHz}$.
  * **L-512 ($512\times 512$)**: Downsampling pooling factor 4, temporal resolution $\Delta t = 3.91\ \mu\text{s}$, frequency resolution $\Delta f = 0.98\text{ MHz}$.
* **Empirical Benchmark Findings**: Evaluated four state-of-the-art detectors (YOLOv3, YOLOv6-M, YOLOv9-M, RT-DETR-L). YOLOv9-M (20.02M parameters, 26.4 GFLOPs) achieved Pareto dominance:
  * At S-128: $1.12\text{ ms}$ inference latency (892.9 FPS on Tesla T4), $85.88\%\text{ mAP}_{50}$ on NIST-CBRS, and $25.93\%\text{ mAP}_{50}$ on RadDet-9T dense multi-target.
  * At L-512: $2.23\text{ ms}$ latency, $47.37\%\text{ mAP}_{50}$ on RadDet-9T dense multi-target (outperforming RT-DETR-L's $29.34\%$ by $+18.03$ percentage points).
* **Core Structural Limitation**: RadDet enforces **static uniform execution**. Frames are locked to a single resolution at deployment. Deploying at S-128 sacrifices $21.44$ percentage points of $\text{mAP}_{50}$ on dense scenes; deploying at L-512 doubles the latency and quadruples memory bandwidth, wasting compute on trivial frames.

### 2.2. RAVEN (CVPR 2026)
* **Architecture**: High-resolution radar tensor learning with spatial-temporal attention mechanisms processing complete 3D Range-Azimuth-Doppler (RAD) tensors.
* **Key Strength**: Preserves raw phase coherence across spatial antenna channels, enabling fine azimuth-Doppler angle estimation.
* **Core Structural Limitation**: The 3D tensor attention backbone requires over $150\text{ GFLOPs}$ per frame and $>200\text{ MB}$ of transient activation memory, vastly exceeding the thermal and SRAM budgets ($10\text{--}15\text{ W}$, $<32\text{ MB}$ on-chip SRAM) of commercial edge microcontrollers (such as TI TDA4VM or Hailo-8).

### 2.3. AdaRadar (CVPR 2026)
* **Architecture**: Adaptive sensor-level chirp rate streaming and physical transceiver modulation. AdaRadar dynamically adjusts the FMCW chirp slope $S = B/T_c$ and pulse repetition interval based on ego-vehicle velocity.
* **Key Strength**: Minimizes physical radio-frequency (RF) transmission power and raw sampling bandwidth at the sensor frontend.
* **Core Structural Limitation**: Requires specialized, software-defined agile radar transmitter hardware capable of nanosecond-level PLL frequency re-tuning. It cannot be retrofitted to standard automotive radar frontend ASICs (e.g., TI AWR2243) that stream standardized, pre-digitized chirp frames. Furthermore, it does not address adaptive neural compute at the perception processor.

### 2.4. Alexander Karlsson's PhD Thesis (Saab Surveillance / KTH Royal Institute of Technology, 2023)
* **Industrial Radar Baseline**: Evaluated on the Saab SIRS 1600 industrial naval/coastal surveillance radar ($f_c = 77\text{ GHz}$, bandwidth $B = 160\text{ MHz}$, chirp duration $T_c = 58.8\ \mu\text{s}$, pulse repetition frequency $f_{\text{prf}} = 17\text{ kHz}$, beamwidth $\theta_{3\text{dB}} = 1.0^\circ$, Taylor antenna weighting $S = -40\text{ dB}$, system noise figure $F = 4.5\text{ dB}$).
* **Four Core Contributions**:
  1. *Stepped-Frequency Pulse Compression*: Non-coherent pulse compression via 1D CNN + MLP overcoming phase distortion in synthetic wideband synthesis.
  2. *Data-Driven Track-Before-Detect (TBD)*: Two-stage CNN on $64\times 32$ Range-Time maps achieving detection probability $P_d > 90\%$ at $3\text{ dB}$ SNR, substantially outperforming classical DP-TBD.
  3. *Micro-Doppler Drone vs. Bird Classification*: Cylindrical synthetic helicopter and bird wing kinematics combined with a latent $k=4$ autoencoder and Quadratic Discriminant Analysis (QDA), achieving $96.4\%$ classification accuracy.
  4. *Low-Angle Naval Target Tracking under Sea Multipath*: Complex monopulse ratio combined with Dual Extended Kalman Filtering to decouple direct and surface-reflected multipath rays.
* **Core Structural Limitation**: Focuses heavily on narrow single-target tracking, micro-Doppler classification, and naval multipath; does not formulate multi-target object detection bounding-box regression or edge computational adaptation.

### 2.5. Dr. Tapas Kumar Pal's Survey (DRDO, IEEE SPACE 2025)
* **Defense Systems Perspective**: Comprehensive evaluation of AI-driven defense radar systems, grounding mathematical radar fundamentals:
  * Complete Radar Range Equation with losses:
    $$P_r = \frac{P_t G_t G_r \lambda^2 \sigma}{(4\pi)^3 R^4 L_{\text{sys}}}$$
    where $L_{\text{sys}} = L_{\text{atm}} L_{\text{rx}} L_{\text{processing}}$ captures atmospheric absorption, beam shape loss, and windowing straddle loss.
  * Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) for non-Gaussian radar sea/ground clutter simulation.
  * Reinforcement Learning (DQN) for cognitive radar electronic counter-countermeasures (ECCM) and adaptive beam steering.
  * Fixed-point 8-bit integer quantization ($w_q = \text{round}(w \cdot 2^n)/2^n$) and gradient saliency ($\partial f(x)/\partial x$) for explainable radar target classification.
* **Core Structural Limitation**: Macro-level system survey without empirical validation on wideband spectrum benchmarks; does not provide concrete convolutional architectures for real-time edge bounding box detection.

### 4.1. Primary Candidate 1: Cascaded Multi-Resolution Triage (Uncertainty-Gated Fast Path)

#### Architectural Mechanism
The triage framework routes incoming radar spectra $x$ through an ultra-fast, lightweight detector $f_{128}(x)$ operating at $128\times 128$ resolution ($1.12\text{ ms}$ latency). An uncertainty gating module $\mathcal{G}(f_{128}(x), x)$ evaluates whether the initial detection meets safety confidence criteria:
* If the frame exhibits low prediction entropy, high estimated SNR, and sparse target density, the prediction $\hat{y}_{128}$ is committed directly to the perception tracker. Approximately $75\text{--}85\%$ of typical driving frames (e.g., open highway) exit at this stage.
* If the gating criteria detect ambiguity (weak targets near the noise floor, target clustering, or elevated classification entropy), the raw frame is escalated to $f_{256}(x)$ or $f_{512}(x)$.

#### Mathematical Routing Policy
Let $x \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$ be the input spectrogram. The discrete routing policy $\pi(x) \in \{128, 256, 512\}$ is governed by:

$$\pi(x) = \begin{cases} 128 & \text{if } \mathcal{H}(\hat{y}_{128}) \le \tau_H \;\wedge\; \widehat{\text{SNR}}(x) \ge \tau_{\text{SNR}} \;\wedge\; \rho(\hat{y}_{128}) \le \tau_\rho \\ 256 & \text{if } \left(\mathcal{H}(\hat{y}_{128}) > \tau_H \;\vee\; \widehat{\text{SNR}}(x) < \tau_{\text{SNR}}\right) \;\wedge\; \rho(\hat{y}_{128}) \le \tau_\rho \\ 512 & \text{if } \rho(\hat{y}_{128}) > \tau_\rho \;\vee\; \mathcal{H}(\hat{y}_{256}) > \tau_H^{\text{high}} \end{cases}$$

where:
1. **Normalized Shannon Classification Entropy**:
   $$\mathcal{H}(\hat{y}) = -\frac{1}{N_{\text{det}} \ln K} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{det}}} \sum_{k=1}^K p_{i,k} \ln p_{i,k}$$
   where $p_{i,k}$ is the predicted softmax class probability for detection $i$ over $K=11$ radar classes.
2. **Estimated Pre-Detection SNR Tier**:
   $$\widehat{\text{SNR}}(x) = 10 \log_{10} \left( \frac{\max_{(u,v)} |x(u,v)|^2}{\frac{1}{|\Omega_{\text{noise}}|} \sum_{(u,v) \in \Omega_{\text{noise}}} |x(u,v)|^2} \right)$$
   where $\Omega_{\text{noise}}$ is the lowest 10th percentile energy region of the spectrogram.
3. **Spatial Target Density**:
   $$\rho(\hat{y}) = \max_{j} \sum_{i \neq j} \mathbb{I}\left( \text{IoU}(B_i, B_j) > 0.1 \right)$$
   measuring overlapping point-spread functions (PSFs) in Doppler-frequency space.

#### Computational Complexity & Latency
The expected latency $\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{T}]$ across the dataset distribution $\mathcal{D}$ is:
$$\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{T}] = P(\pi=128) \cdot \mathcal{T}_{128} + P(\pi=256) \cdot (\mathcal{T}_{128} + \mathcal{T}_{\text{gate}} + \mathcal{T}_{256}) + P(\pi=512) \cdot (\mathcal{T}_{128} + \mathcal{T}_{\text{gate}} + \mathcal{T}_{512})$$
Given empirical RadDet timings on Tesla T4 ($\mathcal{T}_{128} = 1.12\text{ ms}$, $\mathcal{T}_{256} = 1.58\text{ ms}$, $\mathcal{T}_{512} = 2.23\text{ ms}$, $\mathcal{T}_{\text{gate}} \approx 0.05\text{ ms}$):
$$\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{T}] = 0.80(1.12) + 0.15(1.12 + 0.05 + 1.58) + 0.05(1.12 + 0.05 + 2.23) = 0.896 + 0.412 + 0.170 = 1.478\text{ ms}$$
This delivers a **33.7% latency reduction** compared to monolithic L-512 ($2.23\text{ ms}$) while preserving high mAP on the 20% critical frames.

### 4.2. Primary Candidate 2: Cross-Resolution Knowledge Distillation (KD)

#### Architectural Mechanism
Rather than dynamically switching models at runtime, Cross-Resolution KD trains a compact, ultra-fast student model $S_{128}$ ($128\times 128$, $1.12\text{ ms}$) supervised by an expressive, high-capacity teacher network $T_{512}$ ($512\times 512$). 

Because the teacher processes fine-grained spectrogram bins ($\Delta f = 0.98\text{ MHz}$ vs. student's $\Delta f = 3.91\text{ MHz}$), it resolves narrow Doppler sidelobes and subtle chirp curvatures. The distillation pipeline forces the student's intermediate convolutional feature representations to imitate the teacher's representations via spatial-frequency projection adapters.

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                             CROSS-RESOLUTION KNOWLEDGE DISTILLATION                                |
|                                                                                                    |
|  High-Res Input (512x512) ---> [ Teacher T_512 (Frozen) ] ---> P3_T, P4_T, P5_T ---> Head_T       |
|                                                                     |                     |        |
|                                                              Feature Loss          Logit KL Loss   |
|                                                                     |                     |        |
|  Low-Res Input (128x128)  ---> [ Student S_128 (Trainable) ] -> phi(P3), phi(P4) --> Head_S      |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

#### Mathematical Loss Formulation
The overall training objective combines task detection loss, multi-scale intermediate feature mimicry, and temperature-scaled logit matching:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{det}}(y, \hat{y}_S) + \beta_{\text{feat}} \sum_{l \in \{P3, P4, P5\}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{feat}}^{(l)} + \gamma_{\text{logit}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{KD}}^{\text{logit}}$$

1. **Multi-Scale Feature Imitation Loss**:
   $$\mathcal{L}_{\text{feat}}^{(l)} = \frac{1}{2 H_l W_l C_l} \left\| \mathbf{F}_T^{(l)} - \phi_l(\mathbf{F}_S^{(l)}) \right\|_F^2$$
   where $\mathbf{F}_T^{(l)} \in \mathbb{R}^{H_l \times W_l \times C_l}$ is the teacher's feature map at pyramidal stage $l$, $\mathbf{F}_S^{(l)}$ is the student's feature map, and $\phi_l(\cdot)$ is an adaptive projection block consisting of a $1\times 1$ convolution, LayerNorm, and bilinear upsampling to reconcile spatial dimension discrepancies ($4\times$ scale factor).
2. **Frequency-Attention Weighted Feature Loss**:
   Radar energy concentrates in specific Doppler bins. We weight the feature mimicry by spatial-frequency saliency $\mathbf{A}_T^{(l)} = \text{Softmax}\left(\frac{1}{C}\sum_{c=1}^C |\mathbf{F}_{T, c}^{(l)}|^2\right)$:
   $$\mathcal{L}_{\text{salient}}^{(l)} = \left\| \mathbf{A}_T^{(l)} \odot \left( \mathbf{F}_T^{(l)} - \phi_l(\mathbf{F}_S^{(l)}) \right) \right\|_F^2$$
3. **Temperature-Scaled Logit Distillation**:
   $$\mathcal{L}_{\text{KD}}^{\text{logit}} = 2 \mathcal{T}^2 \cdot \text{KL}\left( \sigma\left(\frac{\mathbf{z}_T}{\mathcal{T}}\right) \;\Bigg\|\; \sigma\left(\frac{\mathbf{z}_S}{\mathcal{T}}\right) \right)$$
   where $\mathcal{T} = 4.0$ is the distillation temperature and $\sigma(\cdot)$ is the softmax operator.

#### Systems Advantage
* **Zero Runtime Overhead**: The student model runs at a constant $1.12\text{ ms}$ on edge hardware with zero switching jitter or memory caching penalties.
* **Preserved Resolution**: Transfers the high-frequency chirp boundary recognition of $512^2$ into the fast $128^2$ model.

### 4.3. Primary Candidate 3: Dynamic Layer Early-Exit & Conditional Computation

#### Architectural Mechanism
Dynamic Layer Early-Exit embeds auxiliary detection heads after intermediate stages of the YOLOv9-C backbone (featuring Generalized Efficient Layer Aggregation Networks, GELAN, and Programmable Gradient Information, PGI).

Rather than forcing every frame to traverse all 16 convolutional stages:
* **Stage 2 Exit**: Confident, high-SNR frames with clear line-of-sight targets exit early (consuming only $28\%$ of total backbone FLOPs).
* **Stage 3 Exit**: Moderately complex frames exit after intermediate feature fusion ($58\%$ of FLOPs).
* **Stage 4 (Terminal) Exit**: Dense multi-target frames, low-SNR signals ($-20\text{ dB}$), or multipath clutter propagate through the full deep architecture.

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                         DYNAMIC LAYER EARLY-EXIT BACKBONE TOPOLOGY                                 |
|                                                                                                    |
|  Input x ---> [ GELAN Stage 1 ] ---> [ GELAN Stage 2 ] ---> Exit Head 1  (H(p) < tau_1? Exit!)    |
|                                              |                                                     |
|                                       [ GELAN Stage 3 ] ---> Exit Head 2  (H(p) < tau_2? Exit!)    |
|                                              |                                                     |
|                                       [ GELAN Stage 4 ] ---> Final Head   (Terminal Prediction)    |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

#### Mathematical Formulation
Let $\mathbf{h}_k = f_{\text{stage}_k}(\mathbf{h}_{k-1})$ represent the feature representation at stage $k \in \{2, 3, 4\}$. Each exit head produces class probabilities $p^{(k)}(x)$ and bounding box coordinates $b^{(k)}(x)$.

The exit policy is defined by an entropy threshold vector $\boldsymbol{\tau} = [\tau_2, \tau_3]$:
$$\pi_{\text{exit}}(x) = \min \left\{ k \in \{2, 3, 4\} \mid \mathcal{H}(p^{(k)}(x)) < \tau_k \right\}$$

The joint multi-exit training loss is:
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{early-exit}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{det}}^{(4)}(y, \hat{y}^{(4)}) + \sum_{k=2}^3 \lambda_k \mathcal{L}_{\text{det}}^{(k)}(y, \hat{y}^{(k)}) + \mu \sum_{k=2}^3 \text{KL}\left( p^{(4)}(x) \parallel p^{(k)}(x) \right)$$
where the Kullback-Leibler divergence term enforces semantic consistency between early exits and the final terminal head.

#### Hardware & Cache Benefits
* **Unified Model Weights**: Only one set of model parameters is loaded into edge SRAM/DRAM, eliminating the memory duplication of storing multiple distinct networks.
* **Deterministic Per-Layer Latency**: Each stage execution boundary is fully deterministic, allowing microcontrollers to abort execution at fixed clock cycles.

### 4.4. Exploratory Candidate 4: Self-Supervised RF Pretraining & Representation Learning

#### Architectural Mechanism
Deep learning in radar is severely bottlenecked by the human cost of annotating bounding boxes on complex spectrograms. Methodology 4 leverages massive unannotated radar chirp streams through **Masked Spectrogram Modeling (MSM)**.
* Random patches of the 2.5D spectrogram ($16\times 16$ time-frequency patches) are masked ($75\%$ masking ratio).
* A Vision Transformer (ViT) or ConvNeXt encoder reconstructs the missing complex energy distribution, forcing the network to internalize fundamental RF physical invariants: chirp slope linearity, Doppler dispersion curves, and background noise statistics.
* The pretrained encoder weights are then transferred and fine-tuned on the labeled RadDet dataset.

#### Mathematical Formulation
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{MSM}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}|} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{M}} \left\| x_i - \mathcal{D}_\theta\left( \mathcal{E}_\phi(x \odot (1 - \mathcal{M})) \right)_i \right\|_2^2$$
where $\mathcal{M}$ is the binary patch mask, $\mathcal{E}_\phi$ is the encoder, and $\mathcal{D}_\theta$ is a lightweight reconstruction decoder.

### 4.5. Exploratory Candidate 5: Reinforcement Learning / Learned Policy Allocation

#### Architectural Mechanism
Formulates radar compute allocation as a **Markov Decision Process (MDP)** $\langle \mathcal{S}, \mathcal{A}, \mathcal{P}, \mathcal{R}, \gamma \rangle$:
* **State $\mathbf{s}_t$**: A temporal feature vector combining past detection confidence, vehicle ego-velocity, acceleration, and ambient noise floor estimation across the last $W=5$ frames.
* **Action $a_t$**: Discrete compute allocation $a_t \in \{\text{Low Res / Early Exit}, \text{Medium Res}, \text{Full Res / Full Depth}\}$.
* **Reward $\mathcal{R}_t$**: A balanced reward penalizing detection errors and latency spikes:
  $$\mathcal{R}_t = \text{mAP}_{50}(a_t, x_t) - \lambda_1 \max(0, \mathcal{T}(a_t) - \tau_{\max}) - \lambda_2 \frac{\text{GFLOPs}(a_t)}{\text{GFLOPs}_{\max}}$$
* **Policy Optimization**: Trained via Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with generalized advantage estimation.

## 5. Mathematical Optimization Framework

The theoretical foundation of adaptive radar spectrum detection is formulated through dual lenses: a primal **Constrained Optimization Formulation** and an unconstrained **Multi-Objective Pareto Loss with Lagrangian Relaxation**.

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                THE DUAL OPTIMIZATION FORMULATION                                   |
|                                                                                                    |
|  [ Primal: Constrained Optimization ]               [ Dual: Multi-Objective Pareto Loss ]          |
|                                                                                                    |
|  min  E [ C(x, pi(x)) ]                             L_total = L_det(y, y_hat)                      |
|  s.t. mAP(f(x, pi(x))) >= mAP_oracle - epsilon               + lambda * (C(x) / C_max)             |
|       Latency_P95      <= tau_max                            + gamma * U(x)                        |
|                                                                                                    |
|  (Hard real-time safety guarantees)                 (Continuous gradient-based joint training)     |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 5.1. Primal Constrained Optimization Formulation

In safety-critical automotive radar, accuracy cannot be arbitrarily sacrificed for speed. The system must match the accuracy of an "oracle" high-resolution detector within an acceptable margin $\epsilon$, while strictly adhering to a P95 latency deadline $\tau_{\max}$:

$$\min_{\boldsymbol{\theta}, \boldsymbol{\pi}} \;\; \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathcal{D}} \left[ \mathcal{C}(x, \pi(x)) \right]$$
$$\text{subject to} \quad \begin{cases} \text{mAP}\left(f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(x, \pi(x))\right) \;\ge\; \text{mAP}_{\text{oracle}} - \epsilon \\ \text{P95}_{x \sim \mathcal{D}} \left[ \mathcal{T}(x, \pi(x)) \right] \;\le\; \tau_{\max} \end{cases}$$

where:
* $\mathcal{C}(x, \pi(x))$ denotes the computational operational cost (measured in GFLOPs or energy in Joules).
* $\pi(x) \in \Pi$ represents the adaptive decision policy (resolution selection or layer early exit).
* $\text{mAP}_{\text{oracle}}$ is the performance achieved by the monolithic L-512 detector ($47.37\%\text{ mAP}_{50}$ on RadDet-9T dense multi-target).
* $\epsilon$ is the tolerable performance degradation bound (typically set to $\epsilon \le 1.5\%\text{ mAP}$).
* $\tau_{\max}$ is the parameterized hard real-time latency deadline. Rather than arbitrarily fixing $\tau_{\max}$, we evaluate across the parametric operating interval:
  $$\tau_{\max} \in [1.0\text{ ms}, 5.0\text{ ms}]$$
  where $\tau_{\max} = 2.0\text{ ms}$ represents high-framerate processing ($>500\text{ FPS}$), and $\tau_{\max} = 5.0\text{ ms}$ represents standard $200\text{ FPS}$ automotive frame processing.

### 5.2. Multi-Objective Pareto Loss & Dual Lagrangian Relaxation

To optimize neural parameters $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ and routing parameters $\boldsymbol{\psi}$ simultaneously via end-to-end backpropagation, we formulate the unconstrained Lagrangian relaxation:

$$\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\theta}, \boldsymbol{\psi}; \lambda, \kappa) = \mathbb{E}_{x, y \sim \mathcal{D}} \left[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{det}}\left(y, f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(x, \pi_{\boldsymbol{\psi}}(x))\right) \right] + \lambda \left( \frac{\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{C}(x, \pi_{\boldsymbol{\psi}}(x))]}{\mathcal{C}_{\max}} \right) + \kappa \max\left(0, \text{P95}[\mathcal{T}] - \tau_{\max}\right) + \gamma \mathcal{U}(x)$$

where:
1. **Detection Task Loss**:
   $$\mathcal{L}_{\text{det}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{cls}}^{\text{Focal}}(\hat{p}, p) + \alpha_{\text{box}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{box}}^{\text{CIoU}}(\hat{b}, b) + \alpha_{\text{DFL}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{DFL}}$$
   incorporating Focal Loss for class imbalance across SNR tiers and Complete IoU (CIoU) for bounding box overlap.
2. **Normalized Computational Penalty**:
   $$\lambda \cdot \frac{\mathcal{C}(x, \pi(x))}{\mathcal{C}_{\max}}$$
   penalizes invocations of high-resolution or deep layers.
3. **Uncertainty Regularizer $\mathcal{U}(x)$**:
   $$\mathcal{U}(x) = -\sum_{k=1}^K p_k \ln p_k + \mathbb{D}_{\text{KL}}\left( q(\boldsymbol{\theta}) \parallel p(\boldsymbol{\theta}) \right)$$
   encourages well-calibrated confidence intervals, penalizing overconfident predictions in low-SNR regimes.
4. **Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) Dual Updates**:
   During training, the Lagrange multiplier $\lambda$ is updated dynamically:
   $$\lambda^{(t+1)} = \max\left(0, \lambda^{(t)} + \eta_\lambda \left( (\text{mAP}_{\text{oracle}} - \epsilon) - \text{mAP}^{(t)} \right)\right)$$
   guaranteeing convergence to the Pareto frontier.

### 5.3. Pedagogical Uncertainty Gating: Shannon vs. Evidential Deep Learning

A critical theoretical requirement for adaptive triage is **uncertainty calibration**. If the fast-path detector ($128^2$) produces overconfident erroneous detections on a faint target submerged in $-20\text{ dB}$ noise, the triage gate will fail to escalate the frame. We derive two alternative uncertainty formulations:

#### 1. Shannon Entropy Gating (Information-Theoretic Formulation)
Given class probability vector $\mathbf{p} = [p_1, p_2, \dots, p_K] = \text{Softmax}(\mathbf{z})$, the Shannon entropy measures predictive dispersion:
$$\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{p}) = -\sum_{k=1}^K p_k \ln p_k$$
* *Limitation*: Standard softmax neural networks are notoriously overconfident on out-of-distribution or high-noise inputs because the softmax normalization $\sum p_k = 1$ forces point probabilities even when total evidence is near zero.

#### 2. Evidential Deep Learning (Dirichlet Prior Formulation)
To overcome softmax overconfidence, we formulate uncertainty via **Evidential Deep Learning (EDL)**. Rather than placing a point estimate on class probabilities, the network outputs non-negative evidence parameters $\mathbf{e} = [e_1, \dots, e_K] = \text{Softplus}(\mathbf{z})$, parameterizing a Dirichlet distribution over multinomial probabilities:
$$\text{Dir}(\mathbf{p} \mid \boldsymbol{\alpha}) = \frac{1}{\text{B}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})} \prod_{k=1}^K p_k^{\alpha_k - 1}, \quad \alpha_k = e_k + 1$$
The total Dirichlet strength $S = \sum_{k=1}^K \alpha_k$ represents the total virtual observations gathered by the network.

The predicted class probability and **epistemic uncertainty** $u \in [0, 1]$ are derived in closed form:
$$\hat{p}_k = \frac{\alpha_k}{S}, \qquad u = \frac{K}{S} = \frac{K}{\sum_{k=1}^K (e_k + 1)}$$

* *Triage Gate Property*: When a radar frame contains low-SNR thermal noise, the network outputs small evidence $e_k \approx 0 \implies S \to K \implies u \to 1.0$ (maximum epistemic uncertainty). The gate unconditionally escalates frames with $u > \tau_{\text{evidential}}$, guaranteeing safety fail-safes without requiring heuristic threshold tuning.

### Question 1: Why Deep Learning over Classical Radar DSP (MUSIC, ESPRIT, CA-CFAR) Below 0 dB SNR?

#### Mathematical Failure of Classical Methods
1. **CA-CFAR Target Masking in Heterogeneous Environments**:
   In CA-CFAR, the detection threshold $T$ for the Cell Under Test (CUT) $x$ is:
   $$T = \alpha \cdot \frac{1}{2N} \sum_{i=1}^{2N} x_i$$
   where $\alpha = 2N(P_{\text{fa}}^{-1/2N} - 1)$. In multi-target scenarios or clutter boundaries, adjacent targets spill energy into the reference cells, artificially inflating the noise floor estimate $T$. Consequently, weak valid targets in the CUT are suppressed ($P_d \to 0$), known as **target masking**.
2. **OS-CFAR Sorting & SNR Penalty**:
   Ordered-Statistic CFAR replaces the average with the $k$-th sorted value $x_{(k)}$. While mitigating masking, it introduces an intrinsic **$1.5\text{--}3\text{ dB}$ SNR loss** and requires sorting $O(N \log N)$ per cell, creating severe computational latency.
3. **MUSIC Subspace Breakdown**:
   MUSIC computes the spatial pseudo-spectrum:
   $$P_{\text{MUSIC}}(\theta) = \frac{1}{\mathbf{a}^H(\theta) \mathbf{E}_n \mathbf{E}_n^H \mathbf{a}(\theta)}$$
   Separating the signal subspace $\mathbf{E}_s$ from the noise subspace $\mathbf{E}_n$ requires high SNR ($>10\text{ dB}$). At low SNR ($<0\text{ dB}$), thermal noise eigenvalues swap with signal eigenvalues, contaminating $\mathbf{E}_n$ and producing severe spurious peaks. Furthermore, correlated multipath reflections collapse the rank of covariance matrix $\mathbf{R}_{xx}$; spatial smoothing can restore rank but halves the effective array aperture, degrading resolution back to the Rayleigh limit.

#### The Deep Learning Superiority
* **Empirical Validation (RadDet Table III)**: At $-20\text{ dB}$ SNR on the dense RadDet-9T multi-target benchmark, classical CFAR achieves $P_d < 5\%$, whereas YOLOv9-M achieves **$25.93\%\text{ mAP}_{50}$** at S-128 and **$47.37\%\text{ mAP}_{50}$** at L-512.
* **Implicit Clutter Learning**: Convolutional kernels learn the continuous multi-scale spatial correlation of clutter, effectively performing non-linear adaptive matched filtering across multi-frequency bands without hand-tuned reference windows.
* **Deterministic Forward Execution**: Replaces stochastic, iterative search ($15\text{--}50\text{ ms}$) with a deterministic single-pass tensor contraction ($1.12\text{ ms}$).

### Question 2: How Does Deep Learning Overcome Physical Rayleigh Resolution Limits?

#### Physical Classical Boundaries
For an FMCW radar operating with bandwidth $B = 500\text{ MHz}$, chirp duration $T_c = 50\ \mu\text{s}$, and a 4-element Uniform Linear Array (ULA, $d = \lambda/2$):
* **Range Resolution**: $\Delta R = \frac{c}{2B} = \frac{3 \times 10^8}{2 \times 500 \times 10^6} = 0.30\text{ m}$.
* **Velocity Resolution**: $\Delta v = \frac{\lambda}{2 N_c T_c} \approx 0.304\text{ m/s}$.
* **Angular Rayleigh Limit**: $\Delta \theta \approx \frac{\lambda}{D \cos\theta} = \frac{2}{N_{\text{rx}}-1} \approx 0.667\text{ rad} \approx 38.2^\circ$.

When two targets fall within the same resolution cell, their backscattered returns superimpose coherently:
$$s(t) = A_1 e^{j (2\pi f_1 t + \phi_1)} + A_2 e^{j (2\pi f_2 t + \phi_2)}$$
When $|f_1 - f_2| < \frac{1}{T_c}$, the discrete Fourier transform produces a single merged Dirichlet mainlobe:
$$|S(f)| = \left| A_1 \text{sinc}(\pi(f-f_1)T_c) + A_2 e^{j\Delta\phi} \text{sinc}(\pi(f-f_2)T_c) \right|$$
If $\Delta\phi \approx 0$, the lobe widens into a single ambiguous peak.

#### Deep Super-Resolution & Neural Deconvolution
Deep neural networks break this limit through three learned mechanisms:
1. **Unrolled Learned Deconvolution**: The network acts as an unrolled non-linear deconvolution solver (analogous to Learned FISTA), iteratively inverting the radar Point Spread Function (PSF) $\mathbf{A}$:
   $$\mathbf{x}^{(k+1)} = \text{SoftThreshold}_\lambda \left( \mathbf{x}^{(k)} - \gamma \mathbf{A}^H (\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}^{(k)} - \mathbf{y}) \right)$$
2. **Phase Curvature Exploitation**: While amplitude spectra merge, the phase gradient across adjacent spatial-frequency bins exhibits asymmetric curvature distinct from a single point target. Deep convolutional backbones (RepNCSPELAN) learn these subtle phase skews.
3. **Anchor-Free Continuous Centroid Regression**: Rather than bin-level peak detection, the network regresses continuous target centroids $\tilde{p}_1, \tilde{p}_2$ via Gaussian heatmap heads $Y_{xyc} = \exp\left(-\frac{\|p - \tilde{p}\|^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)$, resolving closely spaced targets separated by sub-bin distances.

### Question 4: Why Use 2.5D Spectrogram Representations Over Raw ADC IQ or 3D RAD Tensors?

#### Representation Hierarchy & Trade-off Analysis
Radar representations exist across four distinct physical levels:
1. **Level 0: Raw Baseband ADC (IQ) Samples** ($\mathbb{C}^{N_{\text{rx}} \times N_{\text{chirp}} \times N_{\text{adc}}}$):
   * Preserves $100\%$ raw physical phase and delay information.
   * *Critical Flaw for DL*: Forcing a neural network to process raw IQ requires dedicating millions of convolutional parameters simply to learn the Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT), a linear operation that hardware FFT engines execute instantaneously in $0.05\text{ ms}$.
2. **Level 1: Range-Doppler Matrix (RDM)** ($\mathbb{R}^{N_v \times N_r}$):
   * Standard 2D FFT output; loses angular spatial dimensions.
3. **Level 2: Full 3D Range-Azimuth-Doppler (RAD) Tensor** ($\mathbb{R}^{N_\theta \times N_v \times N_r}$):
   * Optimal physical representation but requires 3D convolutions ($O(N^3)$ computational scaling), consuming $>150\text{ GFLOPs}$ and exceeding automotive edge thermal limits.
4. **Level 1.5: 2.5D Max-Hold Spectrogram** ($\mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$):
   * **The Optimal Sweet Spot**: Offloads the Range FFT and Doppler FFT to dedicated hardware DSP coprocessors (hardware-accelerated, zero-overhead). The resulting 2.5D max-hold time-frequency representation preserves non-linear chirp energy envelopes and Doppler shifts while matching standard 2D convolutional architectures (YOLOv9, GELAN), maximizing compute efficiency.

### Question 5: How Does the System Generalize Under Severe Noise, Multipath Clutter, and Doppler Ambiguity?

#### Robustness Mechanisms
1. **Multi-SNR Generalization**:
   The RadDet dataset exposes the detector to 6 calibrated SNR regimes from $-20\text{ dB}$ to $+20\text{ dB}$. The convolutional receptive fields aggregate spatial-temporal context over large spectrogram regions ($3\times 3\times L$ effective dilation), acting as an adaptive filter that integrates energy across chirps even when individual time samples fall below the noise floor.
2. **Clutter Suppression without Heuristics**:
   Unlike CA-CFAR, which requires heuristic guard cells to prevent threshold collapse at clutter edges, deep convolutional layers learn spatial clutter textures (e.g., ground reflections, guardrail multipath) directly from training distributions via cross-entropy and CIoU losses.
3. **Doppler Ambiguity Resolution**:
   In FMCW radar, maximum unambiguous velocity is limited by chirp repetition time: $v_{\max} = \frac{\lambda}{4 T_c} \approx 15\text{ m/s}$. Velocities exceeding $v_{\max}$ alias and fold back across the Doppler axis. Deep backbones learn the harmonic folding patterns and contextual associations of vehicles, resolving Doppler ambiguities that cause classical peak trackers to spawn ghost targets.

### Question 6: How Do We Guarantee Stability and Prevent Oscillation in Adaptive Computation?

#### The Policy Oscillation Risk
In adaptive systems where frame resolution or exit depth is conditioned on predicted entropy $\mathcal{H}(x)$, a radar frame whose uncertainty fluctuates near the threshold $\tau$ can induce **policy thrashing** (rapidly oscillating between $128^2$ and $512^2$ on alternating frames). This causes memory allocation churn, cache thrashing, and unpredictable processing jitter.

#### The Triple Stabilization Architecture
To provide formal stability guarantees, we implement three hysteresis mechanisms:
1. **Schmitt Trigger (Dual-Threshold Hysteresis)**:
   Switching between low and high compute requires crossing separated upper and lower bounds:
   $$\pi_t = \begin{cases} \text{High-Res (512)} & \text{if } \mathcal{H}_t > \tau_{\text{high}} \\ \text{Low-Res (128)} & \text{if } \mathcal{H}_t < \tau_{\text{low}} \\ \pi_{t-1} & \text{if } \tau_{\text{low}} \le \mathcal{H}_t \le \tau_{\text{high}} \end{cases}$$
   where $\Delta \tau = \tau_{\text{high}} - \tau_{\text{low}} > 0$ defines an invariant deadband preventing oscillation.
2. **Temporal Exponential Moving Average (EMA) Smoothing**:
   Decisions are conditioned on the temporally smoothed uncertainty $\bar{\mathcal{H}}_t$:
   $$\bar{\mathcal{H}}_t = \alpha_{\text{EMA}} \bar{\mathcal{H}}_{t-1} + (1 - \alpha_{\text{EMA}}) \mathcal{H}_t, \quad \alpha_{\text{EMA}} = 0.85$$
   dampening transient noise spikes from individual corrupted chirps.
3. **Minimum Dwell Time ($\Delta t_{\text{dwell}}$)**:
   Once an escalation to $512^2$ is triggered, the system enforces a minimum dwell window ($N_{\text{dwell}} = 3$ consecutive frames) before de-escalating, ensuring temporal tracking continuity for maneuvering targets.
