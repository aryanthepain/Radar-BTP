# Abstract

Radar sensing is increasingly used in intelligent perception and signal-monitoring applications, but extracting reliable information from radar measurements while respecting computational constraints remains a significant challenge. This study investigates artificial intelligence applications in radar signal processing through two complementary experimental tracks, with an overarching focus on establishing a foundation for resource-efficient radar detection.

The first track develops a synthetic 77 GHz frequency-modulated continuous-wave (FMCW) radar environment and an associated perception pipeline. The simulated complex radar data are processed using range and Doppler Fourier transforms, moving-target-indication clutter suppression, constant-false-alarm-rate detection, angle-of-arrival estimation, and spatial clustering. A custom convolutional neural network is also implemented for joint target classification and coordinate regression across four simulated target categories.

The second track investigates wideband radar-spectrum detection using the RadDet dataset. Time-frequency representations are prepared at S-128 resolution and evaluated using a YOLOv9-C detector across eleven radar signal classes and six signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) levels. In the full-scale experiment, the detector achieves mean mAP@0.5 and mAP@0.5:0.95 values of 25.25% and 19.03%, respectively, across the six SNR bins. The measured mean single-frame GPU inference latency is 1.12 ms. Performance varies substantially with signal quality, with mAP@0.5 increasing from 3.90% at -20 dB SNR to 40.28% at +20 dB SNR, illustrating the difficulty of detection under low-SNR conditions.

The two tracks address distinct radar tasks and are therefore evaluated independently. Together, they establish implementation and benchmarking baselines for further investigation into resource-efficient radar AI. Reducing computational requirements and investigating CPU-oriented inference are identified as complementary directions for future work, rather than completed optimisations. The study provides an experimental basis for selecting and evaluating resource-aware methods in subsequent research.
